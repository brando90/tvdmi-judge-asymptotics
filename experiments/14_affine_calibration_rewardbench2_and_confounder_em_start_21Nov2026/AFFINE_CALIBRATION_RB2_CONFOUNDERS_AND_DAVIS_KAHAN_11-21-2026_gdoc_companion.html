1. What These Results Show and Do Not Show (Explicit Boundaries)

  • Likert Severity Calibration vs. Bradley-Terry Logits: Unanchored SummEval GPT-4 scores retain -1.1039 mean bias (agreement ICC(A,1) = 0.3341 vs. consistency ICC(C,1) = 0.6455). Fitting monotone OLS affine calibration on just m = 5 to 20 human anchor articles eliminates mean bias (-0.0045 to +0.0074), cuts MAE from 1.1295 down to 0.3702, and lifts ICC(A,1) to 0.6441 (p = 4.27e-18 on 5-fold CV). By contrast, Bradley-Terry RLHF reward models in RewardBench 2 already emit well-calibrated cross-entropy margins at m = 0 (RB-Spec ECE = 0.0219, raw logit mean ECE = 0.0134), so tiny m = 5 Platt refits slightly overfit (0.0553) before settling at m = 50 (0.0272).
  • Clean vs. Flooded Tradeoff on RewardBench 2: On clean K = 23 RewardBench 2 where strong families hold a 17-of-23 majority, Naive SVD scores 0.8334 Best-of-4 accuracy while RB-Spec scores 0.7872 because cluster balancing raises weights on weaker legacy singletons. Under M = 32 weak OpenAssistant clones (K = 55), RB-Spec holds 0.7781 while Uniform Mean falls to 0.4803 and Naive SVD to 0.4324 (p = 1.65e-124). Under 54.9% naturally inverted PKU-Alignment/beaver-7b-v1.0-reward judges (K = 51), RB-Spec holds 0.7963 while Naive SVD collapses below random chance to 0.1455 (p = 2.11e-229).
  • Cross-Family Universal Confounders: When every judge family loads onto the same confounder W (e.g., universal length preference), balanced 1/F weighting still stalls at floor c2 vW. Covariate-residualized CR-RB-Spec stays completely invariant across c = 0 to 2.0 (SummEval 0.5936 vs. vanilla RB-Spec 0.2757, p = 6.03e-13; MT-Bench 0.7709 vs. 0.6707, p = 1.59e-04; Monte Carlo 0.8915 vs. 0.5131), assuming observable covariates capture W.
