\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have established a unified theoretical, formal, and empirical characterization
of reference-free evaluation with TVD-MI judge ensembles. Separating
\emph{statistical convergence} from \emph{semantic validity} reveals both why
independent or diverse ensembles converge rapidly and exactly how naive
reference-free pipelines fail in practice.

First, unconditional prompt--response TVD-MI is vulnerable to injective
prompt copying and global label inversions; double-centered oriented peer
contrasts neutralize additive prompt and verbosity shifts while preserving
strict accuracy monotonicity. Second, finite-sample witness maximization creates
substantial winner's-curse overfitting under the null unless decoupled via
split-sample cross-fitting, which also determines the compute-optimal trade-off
$K^\star(B)$ between judge count and sample size. Third, hierarchical cloning
invalidates naive $1/K$ averaging and uniform majority thresholds, whereas our
unsupervised \texttt{RB-Spec TVD-MI} estimator balances redundant families to
recover $1/F$ variance scaling and effective-family robustness up to
$\delta_{\mathrm{fam}} < 1/2$ on both \textsc{SummEval} and \textsc{MT-Bench}.

\textbf{Limitations.} Without a small human anchor set to calibrate score
location, linear/spectral ensembles inherit judge severity offsets (lowering
absolute agreement $\mathrm{ICC(A,1)}$ despite high rank consistency). In addition,
our Lean~4 formalization proves all finite-alphabet variational, invariance,
clone-hijacking, and optimality theorems, while continuous measure-theoretic
generalizations and end-to-end EM IRT fixed-point rates remain open directions.
