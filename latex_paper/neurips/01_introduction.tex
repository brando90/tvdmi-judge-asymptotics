\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Evaluating generated text without access to gold references is a central
problem in modern machine learning and natural language evaluation.
\emph{Reference-free evaluation} replaces human-written references with
automated judges---typically large language models (LLMs)---that score candidate
outputs directly. Empirically, combining multiple judges improves reliability
\citep{verga2024replacing, zheng2023judging}, yet foundational questions remain
unresolved: \emph{when} does scaling judge count $K$ and sample count $n$
converge to true task competence, and \emph{why} do correlated or cloned judge
families cause naive ensembles to stall or invert?

The Total Variation Distance--Mutual Information (TVD-MI) framework
\citep{robertson2025limitations} casts reference-free evaluation as a binary
hypothesis test distinguishing joint prompt--response draws $P^{(c)}_{UV}$
from product draws $P^{(c)}_U \otimes P^{(c)}_V$. Each judge serves as a
bounded witness critic targeting the variational supremum $\tau(c) :=
\tvd(P^{(c)}_{UV}, P^{(c)}_U \otimes P^{(c)}_V)$. Previous studies
analyzed bounded finite-sample robustness \citep{robertson2025limitations},
sparse score-matrix recovery via Item Response Theory (IRT)
\citep{robertson2025tvdmi}, and confounded binary aggregation
\citep{chen2026care}, but left open the joint scaling tradeoffs, in-sample
critic selection biases, hierarchical clone-hijacking failures, and real-LLM
empirical validation.

\textbf{Our contributions.} We present a two-layer theoretical and empirical
framework connecting statistical convergence to semantic validity:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Coverage, consensus, and split-sample two-axis scaling}
  (Theorems~\ref{thm:coverage}, \ref{thm:consensus}, \ref{thm:two-axis}, and
  \ref{thm:split-sample}): We establish $O_p(K^{-1/\beta})$ random-witness
  rates, $2e^{-2K\gamma_0^2}$ consensus decay, the corrected two-sample
  Hoeffding concentration $2Ke^{-n\eta^2}$, and disjoint split-sample
  cross-fitting that eliminates $+\Omega_p(\sqrt{(\log K)/n})$ selection
  inflation and achieves compute-optimal allocation $K^\star(B) \asymp (B/\log B)^{\beta/(\beta+2)}$.
\item \textbf{Semantic validity remedies} (Theorems~\ref{thm:injection-blindness}--\ref{thm:optimal-weights}):
  We prove exact injection blindness ($\tvd = 1 - \sum_x P_X(x)^2$, showing
  why verbatim prompt copying defeats unconditional prompt--response TVD-MI),
  exact invariance of double-centered TVD-MI witnesses to additive prompt and
  verbosity shifts, oriented conditional peer monotonicity, and Nitzan--Paroush
  inverse-variance optimal weighting via weighted Cauchy--Schwarz.
\item \textbf{Hierarchical clone-hijacking falsification and \texttt{RB-Spec TVD-MI}}
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:clone-hijacking}, Theorems~\ref{thm:balanced-1F}--\ref{thm:effective-family-adv}):
  We show that flooding a panel with $K_1 \to \infty$ redundant clones of a
  weak model drives naive averages and SVD toward the weak family floor
  $\tau_1^2$ and can flip adversarial majority signs. Our unsupervised
  \emph{Redundancy-Balanced Spectral Peer TVD-MI} (\texttt{RB-Spec TVD-MI})
  restores family-level $1/F$ error scaling and effective-family adversarial
  tolerance $\delta_{\mathrm{fam}} < 1/2$.
\item \textbf{Real-LLM verification and Lean~4 proofs}
  (Sections~\ref{sec:lean}--\ref{sec:experiments}): We validate every claim on
  held-out \textsc{SummEval} \citep{fabbri2021summeval} (1,600 summaries),
  \textsc{MT-Bench} \citep{zheng2023judging} (2,575 decisive human pairwise
  votes), and \textsc{RewardBench~2} \citep{malik2025rewardbench2} (1,753
  prompts across 27 open-source reward models), and machine-verify all 54
  algebraic, variational, invariance, clone-hijacking, calibration, confounder,
  and Davis--Kahan theorems (28 in \texttt{Basic.lean}, 15 in
  \texttt{NewTheory.lean}, and 11 in \texttt{SpectralCalibration.lean}) in
  Lean~4 without placeholders.
\end{enumerate}
